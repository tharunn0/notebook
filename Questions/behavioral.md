# Behavioral & Leadership — Question Bank (Mid → Senior → Staff Engineer)

Part A covers core behavioral questions asked at every level. Part B covers senior-level ownership, influence, and execution. Part C covers staff-level strategy, organizational impact, and leadership. Part D covers questions about you, your career, and the AI era, plus questions to ask the interviewer.

---

# Part A — Core Behavioral (All Levels)

## 1. Introduction & Background

1. Tell me about yourself.
2. Walk me through your resume and the reasoning behind each move.
3. What is the project you are most proud of, and why?
4. Describe the most technically complex system you have worked on.
5. What does your current team do, and how does your work fit into the company's goals?
6. What are you known for on your team?
7. Why are you looking for a new role right now?
8. Why do you want to work at this company specifically?
9. Why this role, and why at this level?

## 2. Ownership & Accountability

10. Tell me about a time you took ownership of something that wasn't your responsibility.
11. Tell me about a time you noticed a problem nobody else was addressing. What did you do?
12. Describe a time you made a mistake that affected production or customers. How did you handle it?
13. Tell me about a time you missed a deadline. What happened and what did you learn?
14. Tell me about a time you had to deliver bad news to your manager or a stakeholder.
15. Describe a time you followed through on something long after others had moved on.
16. Tell me about a time you owned a system end to end, from design to on-call.
17. Tell me about a time you cut corners. Would you make the same call again?

## 3. Problem Solving & Technical Judgment

18. Tell me about the hardest bug you have ever debugged.
19. Describe a time you had to make a decision with incomplete information.
20. Tell me about a time you simplified a complex system or process.
21. Describe a technical decision you made that turned out to be wrong. How did you find out, and what did you do?
22. Tell me about a time you had to choose between two good technical options. How did you decide?
23. Tell me about a time you pushed back on a technical approach because of its long-term costs.
24. Describe a time you had to learn a new technology or domain quickly to deliver something.
25. Tell me about a time you used data to make or change a decision.
26. Describe a time you solved a problem creatively with limited resources.

## 4. Collaboration & Teamwork

27. Tell me about a time you worked with a difficult teammate.
28. Describe a time you disagreed with a teammate on a technical approach. How was it resolved?
29. Tell me about a time you helped a struggling teammate.
30. Describe a time you worked closely with product, design, or another non-engineering function.
31. Tell me about a time a cross-team dependency put your project at risk.
32. Tell me about a time you had to collaborate with a team in a different time zone or with a different culture.
33. Describe a time you received credit for work that a team did together. How did you handle it?
34. Tell me about a time you had to work with someone whose working style was very different from yours.

## 5. Conflict & Disagreement

35. Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager.
36. Tell me about a time you disagreed with a decision but committed to it anyway.
37. Describe a time you changed your mind after hearing someone else's argument.
38. Tell me about a time a conflict on your team affected delivery. What did you do?
39. Describe a time you had to say no to a stakeholder or a senior leader.
40. Tell me about a time you escalated something. How did you decide escalation was the right move?
41. Describe a time you had to give critical feedback to a peer.

## 6. Feedback & Growth

42. Tell me about the most useful critical feedback you have received. What did you change?
43. Describe a time you received feedback you disagreed with.
44. What is a weakness you are actively working on, and what are you doing about it?
45. Tell me about a time you failed. What did you learn?
46. How do you keep your technical skills current?
47. What is something you have changed your mind about in your engineering career?
48. What would your last manager say is your biggest strength and your biggest area for growth?

## 7. Prioritization & Time Management

49. Tell me about a time you had too many priorities at once. How did you decide what to drop?
50. Describe a time you had to balance shipping fast with doing it right.
51. Tell me about a time requirements changed late in a project.
52. How do you handle interruptions, on-call, and support work alongside project work?
53. Describe a time you said no to work so you could focus on something more important.
54. Tell me about a time you worked under extreme time pressure.

## 8. Customer Focus & Impact

55. Tell me about a time you went beyond the requirements to solve a real customer problem.
56. Describe a time you used customer feedback or usage data to change what you built.
57. Tell me about the work with the biggest measurable impact you have delivered.
58. Describe a time you built something that customers didn't use. What went wrong?
59. How do you decide whether a piece of engineering work is worth doing?

# Part B — Senior Level (Ownership, Influence & Execution)

## 9. Technical Leadership

60. Tell me about a project you led from idea to launch.
61. Describe how you broke down a large, ambiguous project into milestones.
62. Tell me about a time you wrote a design doc that changed the direction of a project.
63. How do you run a design review, and how do you handle strong disagreement in one?
64. Tell me about a time you set technical direction for your team.
65. Describe a time you improved the engineering quality of a team (testing, code review, CI, observability).
66. Tell me about a time you introduced a new technology or practice. How did you get buy-in?
67. Describe a time you stopped a team from adopting a technology you thought was wrong.
68. How do you balance writing code yourself with unblocking and enabling others?

## 10. Ambiguity & Scoping

69. Tell me about a time you were given a vague problem with no clear requirements.
70. Describe a time you had to define the problem before you could solve it.
71. Tell me about a time you reduced the scope of a project to ship something valuable sooner.
72. Describe a time you had to make a call that your manager wasn't available to make.
73. Tell me about a time a project's goals were unclear or conflicting. How did you get alignment?

## 11. Influence Without Authority

74. Tell me about a time you convinced a team you didn't manage to change their approach.
75. Describe a time you built consensus among engineers with strong opinions.
76. Tell me about a time you influenced the product roadmap as an engineer.
77. Describe a time you had to persuade leadership to invest in technical work (refactoring, reliability, migrations).
78. Tell me about a time you failed to persuade someone. What would you do differently?
79. How do you build trust with a new team or a new set of stakeholders?

## 12. Execution & Delivery

80. Tell me about a project that was behind schedule. How did you get it back on track?
81. Describe a time you had to manage risk on a high-stakes launch.
82. Tell me about a large migration you drove. How did you keep it from stalling?
83. Describe how you track progress and communicate status on long-running projects.
84. Tell me about a time you had to cancel or kill a project. How did you make and communicate the decision?
85. Describe a launch that went badly. What did you do in the moment, and afterward?
86. Tell me about a time you shipped something with known technical debt. How did you manage it afterward?

## 13. Reliability, Incidents & Operations

87. Tell me about the worst production incident you were involved in.
88. Walk me through how you led an incident response.
89. Describe a postmortem you ran. What were the action items, and did they get done?
90. Tell me about a time you prevented an outage before it happened.
91. Describe a time you improved on-call health for your team.
92. Tell me about a time you had to balance reliability work against feature work.

## 14. Mentorship & Developing Others

93. Tell me about someone you mentored. How did they grow?
94. Describe a time you helped a junior engineer through a hard problem without solving it for them.
95. How do you onboard a new engineer onto a complex codebase?
96. Tell me about a time you gave someone a stretch opportunity. How did it go?
97. Describe a time you had to help an underperforming teammate.
98. How do you give code review feedback that is direct but not discouraging?

# Part C — Staff Level (Strategy, Organizational Impact & Leadership)

## 15. Technical Strategy & Vision

99. Tell me about a time you defined a multi-year technical vision. How did you get people to buy into it?
100. Describe a strategy you wrote for a problem that spanned several teams.
101. How do you decide which problems are worth staff-level attention?
102. Tell me about a time you identified a risk to the business that nobody else had noticed.
103. Describe a time you aligned technical strategy with company or business strategy.
104. Tell me about a long-term bet you made. Did it pay off?
105. How do you balance innovation with stability across an organization?
106. Tell me about a time you deprecated or consolidated systems across teams.

## 16. Cross-Organizational Influence

107. Tell me about a time you drove alignment across multiple teams or orgs that disagreed.
108. Describe a time you resolved a conflict between two teams with competing priorities.
109. Tell me about a time you influenced a director, VP, or executive decision.
110. Describe how you built a coalition to push through a major technical change.
111. Tell me about a time you had to work against the incentives of another organization.
112. How do you represent engineering concerns in business or product discussions?

## 17. Organizational Health & Engineering Culture

113. Tell me about a time you changed engineering culture beyond your own team.
114. Describe how you set technical standards for an organization without becoming a bottleneck.
115. Tell me about a time you improved developer productivity across many teams.
116. How do you raise the bar for engineering quality across an organization?
117. Describe a time you identified that a team structure was causing technical problems.
118. Tell me about a time you helped grow other senior engineers into staff-level contributors.
119. How do you make sure decisions get written down and remain discoverable?

## 18. Hard Calls & Judgment

120. Tell me about the hardest technical decision you have made. What made it hard?
121. Describe a time you made an unpopular decision. How did you handle the reaction?
122. Tell me about a time you had to choose between two important things that could not both be done.
123. Describe a time you were wrong at a high level. How did you correct course publicly?
124. Tell me about a time you pushed back on a leader's direction because you thought it would hurt the company.
125. How do you decide when to build, when to buy, and when to do nothing?
126. Tell me about a time you had to decide whether a system should be rewritten or incrementally improved.

## 19. Working With Leadership & Managers

127. How do you partner with an engineering manager as a staff engineer?
128. Describe how you handle a situation where your manager and you disagree on priorities.
129. Tell me about a time you helped leadership understand a complex technical trade-off.
130. How do you manage up when you see a problem your leadership doesn't?
131. Describe a time you were asked to do something you believed was wrong.
132. What is the difference between a senior and a staff engineer, in your own words?
133. Which staff archetype fits you best (tech lead, architect, solver, right hand), and why?

# Part D — Motivation, Career & the AI Era

## 20. Motivation, Values & Fit

134. What motivates you at work?
135. What kind of work energizes you, and what drains you?
136. Describe your ideal team and manager.
137. What kind of engineering culture do you do your best work in?
138. Tell me about a time your values conflicted with a business decision.
139. How do you handle stress and avoid burnout?
140. What do you do when you're bored with your work?

## 21. Career & Goals

141. Where do you want to be in three to five years?
142. Do you want to move toward management or stay on the IC track? Why?
143. What skills are you trying to build next?
144. What would make you leave a job?
145. What have you done outside work that made you a better engineer?
146. What is something you built or learned purely out of curiosity?

## 22. Working in the AI Era

147. How do you use AI tools in your day-to-day engineering work?
148. Tell me about a time an AI tool helped you significantly, and a time it led you astray.
149. How do you review and take ownership of AI-generated code?
150. How have AI tools changed how you design, test, or debug systems?
151. How would you introduce AI tools to a team responsibly?
152. How do you keep your fundamentals strong when AI handles more routine work?
153. What do you think separates a great engineer from an average one now that AI can write code?

## 23. Questions to Ask the Interviewer

154. What does success look like in this role after six months and after a year?
155. What are the biggest technical challenges the team faces right now?
156. How are technical decisions made, and who has the final say?
157. How does the company support career growth for senior and staff engineers?
158. What does on-call look like, and how is operational load shared?
159. What is something you would change about engineering here if you could?
160. How is the team using AI in its products and its engineering workflow?
161. Why is this role open, and what happened to the last person in it?

---

## Preparation Checklist (Questions to Ask Yourself)

162. Which eight to ten stories from my career cover the most questions above?
163. Does each story have a clear situation, my specific actions, a measurable result, and a lesson (STAR + L)?
164. Do my stories show impact at the level I'm interviewing for (team, multi-team, organization)?
165. Do I have stories about failure, conflict, and changing my mind, not only successes?
166. Can I tell each story in two minutes and expand into detail when asked?
167. Am I saying "I" for my own contributions and "we" for the team's, clearly separating the two?
168. Do my stories match the company's stated values or leadership principles?
